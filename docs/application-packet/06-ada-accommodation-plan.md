# ADA Accommodation Plan

*Required per 16 TAC §84.40. Describes how APEX provides reasonable accommodations and accessibility aids to students with disabilities, with a particular focus on deaf and hard-of-hearing students.*

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document is APEX's plan for ensuring its online driver education course is accessible to students protected under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, and Texas Government Code Chapter 121. It covers students who are deaf or hard of hearing, blind or low-vision, students with cognitive or learning disabilities, students with motor disabilities, and students with chronic health conditions that affect course participation.

It applies to all instructional content, all assessment activities, all student-facing communication, and all phases of enrollment, instruction, certification, and post-completion records.

---

## 2. Accessibility Standard

APEX's user-facing site and course content target conformance with the **Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA**. New content and features are reviewed against the WCAG checklist before release. Conformance is tested with automated tools (axe DevTools, WAVE) and with manual testing using a screen reader (NVDA or VoiceOver) and keyboard-only navigation.

Where WCAG and TDLR-specific requirements diverge, APEX applies the stricter of the two.

## 3. Accommodations by Disability Category

### 3.1 Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing Students

- **All instructional video and animation content** is delivered with synchronized closed captions in English and Spanish. Captions are reviewed by the instructor of record for accuracy and traffic-law specificity (e.g., "right-of-way" not auto-captioned as "right away"). Captions are turned on by default; the student can toggle them but they remain available throughout.
- **All audio narration** is accompanied by a written transcript published on the same page. Transcripts are time-coded so students can locate specific content.
- **Interactive scenarios with audio cues** (e.g., siren, horn, screech) include a synchronized text label and a visual indicator (icon plus animation). No safety-critical information is delivered through audio alone.
- **Behind-the-wheel coordination** with deaf students is supported by sending parents or designated supervised drivers a written guide; APEX does not require students to receive audio-only instructions during the supervised driving phase.
- **Customer support** is available via text-based channels (email at support@apex.example and an in-app text chat). Phone-only escalation is never required.

### 3.2 Blind and Low-Vision Students

- **All UI elements** are reachable by keyboard, properly labeled for screen readers (ARIA labels, semantic HTML), and rendered in scalable layouts that respect browser zoom up to 200% without loss of functionality.
- **All visual instructional content** (diagrams, signs, scenarios) is accompanied by a descriptive text alternative that conveys the same instructional intent. A blind student studying right-of-way at a four-way stop receives a textual description of the scenario and the same decision tree, rather than a purely visual scenario.
- **Color is never the sole carrier of meaning.** Pass/fail states use icons and text alongside color; traffic-sign training includes shape and text in addition to color.
- **Minimum contrast ratio** is 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for non-text indicators, meeting WCAG 2.1 AA.
- **Behind-the-wheel** is, by Texas law, not available to blind students; APEX directs blind students to the parent-taught program documentation if they were enrolled in error, and offers a full refund.

### 3.3 Students with Cognitive or Learning Disabilities

- **Course pacing** is fully self-paced. The TDLR-required minimum minutes per chapter are enforced as a floor, not a ceiling; a student may spend as much additional time as needed.
- **Quiz retakes** are permitted on the same content. A student may revisit reading material and re-attempt the chapter quiz an unlimited number of times. The TDLR-required 70% mastery score applies, but there is no penalty for multiple attempts.
- **Reading-level guidance**: course content is written at approximately a 9th-grade reading level, audited annually. Glossary tooltips on traffic-law terminology are available on every page.
- **Distraction-reduction mode** (a high-contrast, animation-free, text-only view) is available from the global settings menu. This view preserves all instructional content and meets the same mastery requirements.

### 3.4 Students with Motor Disabilities

- **Full keyboard navigation** is supported across every page; no part of the course requires mouse use.
- **No timed interactions** are presented in the course content except where TDLR rules require them (e.g., the 90-second identity-validation response window per §84.501). For required timed interactions, APEX provides an "extended time" accommodation on documented request, raising the window to 180 seconds in line with common educational-testing accommodations.
- **Drive-simulator scenes** are available in a non-interactive, observation-only mode for students who cannot operate the keyboard controls. Mastery questions about the scenario remain the same in either mode.

### 3.5 Students with Chronic Health Conditions

- **Course access does not expire** as long as the provider license remains active. A student dealing with a hospitalization or extended treatment can pause progress and resume without losing credit.
- **Identity-validation lockouts** triggered during a flare-up or medical event can be reopened with documentation forwarded to the instructor of record.

## 4. Accommodation Request Process

A student requesting accommodation beyond the defaults above follows this process:

1. The student sends a request to **support@apex.example** describing the accommodation needed and providing documentation from a qualified professional (medical doctor, audiologist, optometrist, licensed psychologist, or qualified educational diagnostician). APEX accepts documentation from the student's school's Section 504 coordinator or IEP team where applicable.
2. APEX's compliance officer (initially the founder; ultimately the instructor of record) reviews the request within **5 business days** and responds in writing with either (a) approval and a description of the accommodation, (b) a request for additional documentation, or (c) a denial with a written explanation and a description of an alternative accommodation the school can offer.
3. If approved, the accommodation is recorded in the student's profile and applied automatically going forward.
4. A student may appeal a denial by writing to support@apex.example and requesting review. Appeals are reviewed by the instructor of record within 10 business days.

No additional fee is charged for any accommodation.

---

## 5. Spanish-Language Accessibility

While Spanish-language support is not strictly an ADA accommodation, APEX is built Spanish-native: every page, every quiz question, every scenario, and every certificate-status message is available in Spanish. Captions, transcripts, and accessibility features described above apply equally in both languages.

---

## 6. Records and Audit

APEX retains records of every accommodation request and outcome for **three years** in line with §84.81 record-retention requirements. Records include the request date, the documentation submitted, the accommodation approved or denied, and the staff member who handled the request. TDLR's authorized representatives may request these records during an inspection or audit.

---

## 7. Continuous Improvement

This plan is reviewed annually by APEX leadership and the instructor of record. Changes to TDLR rules, WCAG guidance, or student feedback that surface accessibility gaps drive updates. The most recent revision date is recorded at the top of this document.
