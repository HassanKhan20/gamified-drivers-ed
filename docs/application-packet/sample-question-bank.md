# Sample Question Bank (Excerpt)

*Submitted as a representative excerpt of APEX's comprehensive final exam and per-module assessment question banks. The full bank contains in excess of 200 questions across the 12 modules; this excerpt presents 25 questions covering all 12 modules for review.*

## Module 1 — Driver Education & the Highway Transportation System

**1.** How many hours of TDLR-credited instruction does the Texas teen driver education course require?
- A) 16
- B) 24
- C) 32 ✓
- D) 40

*Why:* Per 16 TAC §84.501, online (AMI) driver education must present at least 1,920 minutes (32 instructional hours) of content.

---

**2.** The minimum age to apply for a Texas learner permit is:
- A) 14
- B) 15 ✓
- C) 16
- D) 17

*Why:* Texas law permits learner permit applications at age 15 after completing 6 hours of classroom driver education.

---

**3.** A teen with a Texas provisional license may carry how many non-family passengers under age 21?
- A) Zero
- B) One ✓
- C) Two
- D) No limit

*Why:* Texas provisional license restrictions limit drivers to a maximum of one non-family minor passenger; crash rates rise sharply with each additional teen passenger.

## Module 2 — Driving Tasks & Procedures

**4.** Before changing lanes, you should:
- A) Signal, check your mirrors, glance over your shoulder, then change lanes ✓
- B) Change lanes quickly, then signal
- C) Wait until traffic clears for a quarter mile, then move over
- D) Signal and change lanes simultaneously

*Why:* Texas Transportation Code requires signaling intent at least 100 feet before any lane change; the mirror + shoulder check is required because mirrors do not eliminate blind spots.

---

**5.** The "three-second rule" for following distance applies:
- A) Only on highways
- B) Only in clear weather
- C) As a minimum in dry conditions; more in adverse conditions ✓
- D) Only when you are a new driver

*Why:* The three-second following distance is a minimum baseline that should expand under rain, fog, or heavy traffic.

## Module 3 — The Vehicle: Operation, Control & Maintenance

**6.** Hydroplaning is most likely to begin at what speed on wet roads?
- A) 15 mph
- B) 25 mph
- C) 35 mph ✓
- D) 65 mph

*Why:* Most passenger vehicles can begin to hydroplane around 35 mph on standing water; this is why reducing speed in rain is critical.

---

**7.** If your vehicle begins to skid, you should:
- A) Brake hard immediately
- B) Steer in the direction you want the front of the car to go ✓
- C) Turn the wheel away from the skid
- D) Accelerate to regain control

*Why:* Steering into the skid (the direction the rear is sliding) is the standard recovery technique taught by every defensive driving program.

## Module 4 — Driver Readiness (Community Safety Education Act, Driving with Disabilities)

**8.** During a traffic stop, the Community Safety Education Act (TX HB 2305) requires driver education to cover:
- A) How to argue your case with the officer
- B) The proper procedure for both drivers and officers during a stop ✓
- C) How to record the officer
- D) Texas-specific search-and-seizure law

*Why:* HB 2305 (2017) mandates that driver education explain the duties of both the driver and the officer during a traffic stop, focused on de-escalation and clear expectations.

---

**9.** The Texas Driving with Disabilities Program (TX HB 1554, §521.142(g)) is:
- A) A penalty for drivers with disabilities
- B) A voluntary registry that informs law enforcement of a driver's or passenger's disability before a stop ✓
- C) A medical waiver for drivers with conditions that prevent driving
- D) A federal program

*Why:* The voluntary registry, accessed at register.dps.texas.gov, lets drivers communicate to first responders that they or a passenger has autism, hearing loss, PTSD, or similar conditions, reducing miscommunication.

## Module 5 — Risk Reduction (Street Racing, Work Zones)

**10.** Under TX Transportation Code §545.420, organized street racing on a public roadway is at minimum a:
- A) Traffic infraction with a fine
- B) Class B misdemeanor ✓
- C) Felony in all cases
- D) Civil violation

*Why:* §545.420 makes racing on a highway a Class B misdemeanor on the first offense, escalating to a Class A misdemeanor and beyond on subsequent offenses or when injury occurs.

---

**11.** Effective May 1, 2026 (SB 1366), the fine for a speeding violation in an active work zone where workers are present is:
- A) The standard speeding fine
- B) Doubled ✓
- C) Tripled, plus jail time
- D) Suspended at the officer's discretion

*Why:* SB 1366, adopted by TDLR on April 29, 2026 with effective date May 1, 2026, strengthened existing work-zone penalty enhancements; fines double when workers are present, with criminal penalties (state jail felony or higher) if a worker is injured.

## Module 6 — Environmental Factors

**12.** Driving directly into a low sun glare, your safest immediate action is to:
- A) Speed up to get past the glare zone
- B) Lower your visor, slow down, and increase following distance ✓
- C) Switch on your high beams
- D) Close one eye

*Why:* Glare reduces visibility and reaction time; the safe response is to reduce speed, lower the sun visor, and create more space ahead.

---

**13.** In foggy conditions, you should use:
- A) High beams to penetrate the fog
- B) Low beams or fog lights ✓
- C) Hazard lights while driving
- D) No lights at all

*Why:* High beams reflect off fog droplets and reduce visibility further. Low beams (or fog lights, which are angled lower) are the correct choice.

## Module 7 — Distractions

**14.** Under Texas law for drivers under 18, using a wireless device while driving is:
- A) Allowed for music only
- B) Allowed at speeds under 35 mph
- C) Prohibited entirely ✓
- D) Allowed if hands-free

*Why:* The provisional license (under 18) bars all wireless device use while driving, including hands-free, except for emergency calls.

---

**15.** Reading a text message while driving at 55 mph means your eyes leave the road for approximately:
- A) 1 second, covering ~80 feet
- B) 2 seconds, covering ~120 feet
- C) 5 seconds, covering ~400 feet ✓
- D) Less than half a second

*Why:* The average text-read time is about 5 seconds, which at 55 mph covers approximately the length of a football field — long enough for a hazard to materialize without you seeing it.

## Module 8 — Alcohol and Other Drugs

**16.** Texas zero-tolerance law for drivers under 21 means a BAC of:
- A) Any detectable amount results in penalty ✓
- B) Below 0.04 is permitted
- C) Below 0.08 is permitted
- D) Below 0.10 is permitted

*Why:* For drivers under 21, any detectable alcohol in the bloodstream is grounds for DUI charges under Texas zero-tolerance law.

---

**17.** Texas's implied consent law (§724.011) means that by holding a Texas driver license, you have already consented to:
- A) Driver education
- B) Vehicle searches
- C) Chemical testing (breath/blood/urine) if arrested for DWI ✓
- D) Annual eye exams

*Why:* §724.011 establishes that license holders implicitly consent to chemical testing during a lawful DWI arrest; refusal triggers an automatic license suspension.

## Module 9 — Adverse Conditions

**18.** On an icy road, your stopping distance compared to dry pavement is approximately:
- A) The same
- B) Double
- C) Five to ten times longer ✓
- D) Slightly shorter

*Why:* On ice, friction drops dramatically; stopping distances can be 5–10× normal, and braking too hard can lock the wheels and lengthen the stop further.

---

**19.** In a thunderstorm with reduced visibility and heavy rain, the safest action is to:
- A) Pull off into the rightmost lane and stop
- B) Pull onto the shoulder and wait until visibility improves ✓
- C) Maintain highway speed with hazard lights on
- D) Drive in the center lane between two large trucks

*Why:* If visibility is so reduced that you cannot see ahead clearly, pull completely off the roadway (not the shoulder of an active highway lane) and wait it out.

---

## Module 10 — Vehicle Requirements

**20.** The Texas tire-tread minimum legal depth is:
- A) 1/32 inch
- B) 2/32 inch ✓
- C) 4/32 inch
- D) 8/32 inch

*Why:* Texas inspection rules require a minimum of 2/32 inch tread depth; below this the tire will fail inspection and is unsafe in rain.

---

**21.** A motor vehicle inspection (state safety inspection) on a passenger vehicle in Texas is required:
- A) Monthly
- B) Annually ✓
- C) Every five years
- D) Only when sold

*Why:* Texas requires an annual safety inspection for passenger vehicles, with the inspection sticker shown on the windshield.

## Module 11 — Consumer Responsibilities (Human Trafficking)

**22.** As a driver, signs that may indicate human trafficking at a rest area, gas station, or truck stop include:
- A) Out-of-state license plates
- B) A person who seems disoriented, fearful, or unable to speak for themselves; controlled movement by another person ✓
- C) Heavy luggage
- D) A young person traveling alone

*Why:* TX SB 9 and TEC §1001.107 mandate driver education on trafficking indicators: a person under another's control, signs of physical abuse, disorientation, or scripted responses are warning signs. Drivers should not intervene directly but call the National Human Trafficking Hotline at **1-888-373-7888** or 911.

---

**23.** If you suspect human trafficking while driving, the correct action is:
- A) Approach and ask the suspected victim if they need help
- B) Confront the suspected trafficker
- C) Call 1-888-373-7888 (National Human Trafficking Hotline) or 911, and provide details of what you observed ✓
- D) Post a description on social media

*Why:* Direct intervention can endanger the victim. The proper response is to report observations to trained authorities who can investigate safely.

## Module 12 — Personal Responsibilities (Anatomical Gifts)

**24.** Under TX Transportation Code §521.401, when applying for or renewing a Texas driver license, you are asked:
- A) Whether you want to register as an organ donor ✓
- B) Whether you have a medical condition
- C) Whether you have ever donated blood
- D) Whether you want to enroll in the disability registry

*Why:* §521.401 requires DPS to ask about organ donor registration at every license application or renewal; saying yes adds you to Donate Life Texas, the official state registry.

---

**25.** A common myth about organ donation is that:
- A) Doctors will not try as hard to save your life if you are a registered donor — this is false ✓
- B) Donors must be in perfect health — partially false
- C) Donation costs the donor's family money — partially false
- D) Religious traditions universally permit donation — varies

*Why:* The myth that medical staff will provide less care to a registered donor is the most widespread and damaging misconception. In Texas, the medical team treating you is separate from the team that handles donation; their sole obligation is to save your life.
